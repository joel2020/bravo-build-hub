If you live in a 3,500+ sq ft Chappaqua (10514) center-hall colonial and the upstairs bedrooms are 8°F warmer than the kitchen in summer, your system isn't broken — you need zoning.

## What zoning actually does

A zoning system uses motorized dampers in your ductwork to direct air to specific floors or rooms based on multiple thermostats. Instead of one temperature for the whole house, you get 2–4 independent zones.

## Typical Chappaqua home → typical zone setup

- **2-story 3,000–4,000 sq ft:** 2 zones (upstairs / downstairs)
- **3-story or large 4,000–6,000 sq ft:** 3 zones (upstairs / main / basement-or-bonus)
- **Estate homes 6,000+ sq ft:** 4+ zones, often with separate equipment

## Why retrofitting Chappaqua homes can be tricky

- **Old ductwork may not support dampers** — flex duct collapses around motorized dampers; rigid trunks needed
- **Bypass damper required** — without it, closed zones cause static pressure spikes
- **Variable-speed blower strongly recommended** — single-speed blowers struggle with zoning
- **Basement HVAC closet access** — Chappaqua basements vary wildly

## When zoning is the wrong fix

- Air handler is undersized — you need a bigger system, not zones
- Ductwork is leaking 30%+ — seal first, zone after
- Original install used long undersized runs to bedrooms — sometimes adding a mini-split is cheaper

## When zoning is the right fix (most Chappaqua cases)

- One thermostat, dramatic temperature swings between floors
- 3,000+ sq ft, single-system home
- Existing variable-speed equipment
- Family with different temperature preferences (master bedroom cold, kid rooms warm, etc.)
