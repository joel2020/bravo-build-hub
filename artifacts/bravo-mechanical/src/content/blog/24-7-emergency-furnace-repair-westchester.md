## Step 1: Check these 4 things before you call

Some no-heat calls have a simple cause. Only check items that are safe and accessible:

1. **Thermostat** — set to HEAT and set above the current room temperature
2. **Furnace switch** — the service switch near the unit should be ON
3. **Breaker** — check whether the heating circuit has tripped
4. **Filter** — a severely clogged filter can restrict airflow and cause shutdowns

If the system still will not run, keeps shutting off, smells unusual, or trips power repeatedly, call for service.

## Step 2: While you wait — keep the house safe

- Keep interior doors open to help heat circulate
- Open cabinet doors near plumbing on exterior walls
- Use space heaters only according to manufacturer instructions
- Keep portable heaters away from bedding, curtains, furniture, and pets
- Never use a stove, oven, charcoal grill, or outdoor heater for indoor heat
- Make sure carbon monoxide alarms are working

## What can affect emergency repair cost

Emergency furnace repair pricing varies by the diagnostic findings and whether parts are available. Common cost drivers include:

- After-hours or weekend timing
- Igniter, flame sensor, pressure switch, blower, inducer, or control-board issues
- Electrical or thermostat problems
- Restricted airflow or dirty components
- Equipment age and parts availability
- Whether a temporary repair or full replacement is needed

## When it is a 911 call, not an HVAC call

Call **911 immediately** if:

- A carbon monoxide detector is alarming
- You smell gas or rotten eggs
- There are visible flames outside the burner area
- You have dizziness, nausea, headaches, or flu-like symptoms that improve when you leave the home
