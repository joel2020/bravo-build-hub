Rye (10580) has a mix of historic colonials, mid-century waterfront homes, and condos in Rye Brook — almost none of which have central ductwork. Ductless mini-splits are usually the right answer.

## Why ductless works in Rye

- **No ductwork required** — preserves historic interiors
- **Quiet** — important in dense lots near the Sound
- **Highly efficient** — modern cold-climate units perform down to -5°F
- **Heating + cooling in one system** — qualifies for NYS Clean Heat rebates
- **Salt-air corrosion-resistant outdoor units** available for waterfront homes (Milton Point, Greenhaven)

## Typical Rye project sizing

| Home size | Typical zones | Equipment range |
|---|---|---|
| 1,200–1,800 sq ft | 2–3 zones | $10,000–$16,000 |
| 1,800–2,800 sq ft | 4–5 zones | $15,000–$24,000 |
| 2,800–4,500 sq ft | 6–8 zones | $22,000–$36,000 |

## Timeline for a typical Rye install

- **Day 1:** site survey, measurements, load calculation (Manual J)
- **Week 1–2:** equipment ordered, City of Rye permit pulled
- **Day of install (1–3 days):** mounting brackets, line sets, electrical, indoor heads, commissioning
- **Week 4:** Rye electrical inspection sign-off

## Sound Shore-specific considerations

- **Salt-air protection coating** ($300–$600 upgrade) — strongly recommended within 1 mile of the Sound
- **Hurricane mounting** — bolted, not strapped, for shoreline homes
- **Flood zone equipment elevation** — outdoor unit on stand, not slab, in AE/VE zones

## Rebates that apply in Rye

- **NYS Clean Heat:** $1,500–$3,000 per ton of capacity
- **Con Edison:** $200–$500 per indoor head
- **Federal 25C:** up to $2,000 for qualifying heat pumps

A typical 5-zone Rye install runs **$19,500 before rebates, $13,000–$15,000 after.**
