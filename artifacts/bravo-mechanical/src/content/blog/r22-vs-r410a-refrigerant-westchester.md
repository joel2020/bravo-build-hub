**Quick answer:** **R-22 (Freon)** was banned for new production in 2020 and now costs $150–$250/lb to recharge — replace the system instead. **R-410A** is being phased down in 2025 under the AIM Act and replacement equipment is increasingly hard to source. **R-454B** is the current standard for new installs in 2025+ Westchester homes.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Refrigerant | Status (2025) | Used in | Cost per lb | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-22 (Freon) | Banned for new production since 2020 | AC units installed before ~2010 | $150–$250 | Replace the system |
| R-410A (Puron) | Production phasing down 2025 | AC units 2010–2024 | $90–$160 | Repair OK; plan for replacement |
| R-454B (Opteon XL41) | Current standard (since Jan 2025) | New installs 2025+ | $70–$110 | Standard going forward |
| R-32 | Common in mini-splits | Ductless systems | $60–$95 | Standard for ductless |

## Why your old AC suddenly costs so much to repair

If your Westchester AC was installed before ~2010, it almost certainly uses R-22. A leak repair on an R-22 system in 2025 typically runs:

- Diagnosis + leak detection: $220–$385
- R-22 recharge (3–6 lbs typical): $450–$1,500
- Same leak next season (if not properly fixed): repeat the bill

At that point, replacement makes more financial sense than repair.

## R-410A in 2025 — what's actually happening

The EPA's AIM Act caps HFC production. R-410A equipment manufactured before Jan 1, 2025 can still be installed and serviced for years. But:

- New R-410A condensers stopped shipping in early 2025
- Refrigerant supply for service is fine through ~2030
- Parts for R-410A systems will get harder to find after 2027

If your R-410A system is 8+ years old and needs a major repair (compressor, coil), replacement is usually the smarter call.
