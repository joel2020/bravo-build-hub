Irvington (10533) has one of the most architecturally significant housing stocks in Westchester — 1850s–1910s Victorian, Italianate, and Queen Anne homes lining the Hudson River bluffs. Adding modern HVAC without ruining 9-foot ceilings, plaster walls, or original woodwork is genuinely hard. Here's what works.

## Option 1 — Ductless mini-splits (best for most Irvington Victorians)

**Pros:**
- No ductwork through plaster walls
- Small (3-inch) refrigerant penetrations only
- Heating + cooling in one
- NYS Clean Heat rebate eligible
- Wall, ceiling, or floor-mounted indoor heads available

**Cons:**
- Indoor heads visible (though slim, modern designs are improving)
- Outdoor units need careful placement (often hidden behind shrubs or in side yards)

**Typical Irvington Victorian install:** $18,000–$32,000 for 5–7 zones.

## Option 2 — High-velocity small-duct (Unico, SpacePak)

**Pros:**
- 2-inch flexible ducts snake through wall cavities and ceiling soffits
- Tiny round outlets (5-inch) preserve aesthetics
- Heating + cooling
- Quiet, even temperature distribution

**Cons:**
- More expensive than ductless
- Not all techs know how to install correctly
- Higher operating cost than ductless heat pumps

**Typical Irvington Victorian install:** $26,000–$42,000.

## Option 3 — Restore steam/hot water + add separate AC

For homes where the original radiators are aesthetic features, restore them and add a separate cooling system.

**Approach:**
- New high-efficiency boiler ($11,000–$16,000)
- Mini-splits or high-velocity for cooling only ($14,000–$28,000)
- Total: $25,000–$44,000

## What we don't recommend in Irvington Victorians

- **Conventional ducted central AC** — requires soffits, lost ceiling height, drywall through plaster
- **Window units** — often prohibited by Historic District guidelines, plus aesthetic ruin
- **Outdoor units on Hudson-facing facades** — visibility issues with the Historic Society

## Irvington Historic District considerations

If your home is in the Irvington Historic District (most of the village west of Broadway), exterior changes need Historic District Review. That includes:
- Outdoor heat pump unit placement
- Refrigerant line routing on exterior walls
- Vent terminations
- Exterior wall penetrations

We coordinate with the Historic District Review Committee for our Irvington customers — most projects approved with thoughtful placement.

## Energy considerations

Old Irvington homes leak air. The single highest-ROI move is **air sealing + insulation + storm windows BEFORE upgrading HVAC.** A leaky Victorian with a brand-new heat pump will still be expensive to heat. Coordinate with a local energy auditor for NYS Comfort Home rebates that stack with HVAC incentives.
