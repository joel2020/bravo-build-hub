**Quick answer:** Yes, every Westchester County, NY municipality requires a permit for HVAC installation work — including furnaces, boilers, heat pumps, AC condensers, mini-splits, ductwork, gas piping, and water heaters. Routine repairs and tune-ups do not require a permit. **Only a licensed Westchester County contractor can pull the permit** — homeowners cannot self-permit HVAC work.

## What requires a permit (every Westchester town)

- New furnace, boiler, or heat pump installation
- AC condenser replacement (yes, even like-for-like)
- Ductless mini-split installation
- New ductwork or major modifications
- Gas piping (any new run or extension)
- Oil tank installation or removal
- Water heater replacement (gas or electric)
- Indoor air handler replacement
- Chimney lining or relining

## What doesn't require a permit

- Filter changes
- Thermostat replacement (like-for-like)
- Capacitor or contactor replacement
- Routine tune-ups and maintenance
- Refrigerant top-off (with leak repair)
- Blower motor replacement
- Flame sensor cleaning

## What inspections to expect

Most residential HVAC jobs get two inspections:

1. **Rough inspection** — gas piping, venting, electrical, condensate routing before insulation/closing walls
2. **Final inspection** — fire-up, combustion analysis, CO test, equipment match to permit

A **Certificate of Compliance** is issued once it passes.

## Why this matters at resale

Westchester real-estate attorneys flag unpermitted mechanical work during title searches. We've seen closings delayed 60+ days because a previous owner installed a boiler without a permit. Resolving it after the fact means:

- Open inspections of completed work
- Possible re-work to current code
- Back-fees and penalties
- Sometimes full equipment removal and reinstall

This costs sellers $2,000–$15,000+ at closing time.

## Red flags from contractors

- "We don't pull permits — saves you money"
- "You pull the homeowner permit yourself" (illegal in Westchester for HVAC)
- Quote that doesn't itemize the permit
- "We can do the work, then permit it later"
- "Inspection will fail — we'll just skip it"
