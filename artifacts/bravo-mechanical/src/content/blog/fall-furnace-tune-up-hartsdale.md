Hartsdale (10530) homes lean toward 1940s–1970s ranches, capes, and split-levels — most with original ductwork and frequently-replaced furnaces. A real fall tune-up catches problems before the first cold snap.

## What a real Hartsdale tune-up includes

### Combustion safety (the most important part)
1. Test for CO around the heat exchanger
2. Inspect heat exchanger with mirror or borescope
3. Verify draft / flue pressure
4. Check vent pipe integrity
5. Confirm gas line pressure (static and operating)

### Burner and ignition
6. Clean burners
7. Adjust burner air shutter if needed
8. Clean flame sensor (#1 cause of "won't stay lit" calls)
9. Verify ignition timing
10. Inspect igniter ohms

### Blower and airflow
11. Lubricate motor (if older PSC) or check ECM diagnostics
12. Clean blower wheel if dirty
13. Check belt tension (rare on modern units)
14. Measure blower amp draw
15. Verify static pressure across system

### Electrical and controls
16. Tighten all electrical connections
17. Test capacitor (microfarad reading)
18. Test sequencer (if applicable)
19. Verify thermostat calibration

### Filtration and condensate
20. Replace 1" filter or inspect 4–5" media filter
21. Flush condensate (high-eff units) and test float switch

### Final
22. Run full heating cycle, document supply/return temp split (should be 35–70°F depending on furnace size)

## What should NOT be in a tune-up bill

- "Refrigerant top-off" (that's AC, not furnace)
- "Coil cleaning" (also AC, not relevant in fall)
- "Disinfectant fog" (not standard, often unnecessary)
- "Lifetime warranty for $X" upsells
