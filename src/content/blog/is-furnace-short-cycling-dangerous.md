**Quick answer:** Furnace short cycling — turning on and off every 2–5 minutes — is usually **not immediately dangerous** but it can be a symptom of something that is, including a cracked heat exchanger, blocked flue, or failing high-limit switch. **Shut the furnace off immediately if** you smell gas, smell exhaust inside the home, see soot near vents, hear a CO alarm, or see flames outside the burner box.

## The 5 emergency-shutoff signs

Turn the furnace off at the emergency switch and call a tech (or 911 for gas/CO) if you see:

1. **Yellow or orange burner flames** (should be steady blue)
2. **Soot or black streaking** near supply registers or the furnace itself
3. **Sweet, oily, or rotten-egg smell** in the home
4. **Active CO alarm**
5. **Visible flames outside the burner enclosure**

Otherwise, it's safe to keep running while you wait for service.

## What's actually causing the short cycling

| Cause | Severity | DIY fix? |
|---|---|---|
| Dirty air filter (overheating high-limit) | Medium | Yes — replace it |
| Closed/blocked supply registers | Low | Yes — open them |
| Oversized furnace | Long-term | No — sizing redo |
| Cracked heat exchanger | **High — CO risk** | No — replace |
| Blocked flue / vent | **High — CO risk** | No — clear immediately |
| Failing flame sensor | Low | Maybe — clean it |
| Bad thermostat / wiring | Medium | No |
| Failing pressure switch | Medium | No |
| Failing high-limit switch | Medium | No |

## The cracked heat exchanger problem

This is the one that can actually be dangerous. A cracked heat exchanger lets combustion gases — including carbon monoxide — leak into the air your blower pushes through the house. Symptoms:

- Soot near supply vents
- Persistent flu-like symptoms (headache, dizziness) that improve when you leave the house
- CO detector chirping or alarming
- Yellow burner flame
- Rust or scale on the burner deck

If a tech identifies a cracked heat exchanger, **the furnace gets red-tagged immediately**. That's not optional.

## Westchester-specific risks

- **Pre-1995 furnaces** (still common in Yonkers, Mount Vernon, New Rochelle) — heat exchangers aging out
- **Tightly sealed newer homes** in Scarsdale, Larchmont, Bronxville — small leaks build CO faster
- **Down-vented basement furnaces** with bird-nest blockages in spring/fall

## What you should do right now

1. Check that you have a **working CO detector on every floor** (replace every 5–7 years).
2. Replace the air filter if it's been more than 60 days.
3. Confirm all supply registers are open.
4. If short cycling continues — **schedule a combustion analysis**, not just a tune-up. A real diagnosis includes CO ppm readings at the flue and at supply vents.
