Pelham (10803) and Pelham Manor (10803) are full of 1920s–1940s homes on steam or hot-water radiator systems. When one radiator goes cold, it's almost never the boiler — it's the radiator itself. Here's how to diagnose.

## First — figure out what kind of system you have

- **One pipe coming into each radiator + an air valve on the side** = single-pipe steam
- **Two pipes per radiator** = two-pipe steam OR hot water
- **Pressure gauge on boiler reads 0–2 psi** = steam
- **Pressure gauge reads 12–25 psi** = hot water (hydronic)

## Single-pipe steam — the #1 cause: dead air valve

The little brass or chrome valve on the side of the radiator (the one that hisses) is a vent. When it fails closed, steam can't push air out, and the radiator stays cold. **$15–$25 part, 5-minute fix** if you're handy.

How to test: Remove the valve while the system is calling for heat. If steam now reaches the radiator, the valve was bad.

## Two-pipe hot water — the #1 cause: trapped air

Hot water radiators need to be **bled** annually. The bleeder is a small square or screwdriver-slot valve at the top of the radiator.

1. Turn off the boiler
2. Hold a cup under the bleeder
3. Open the valve until water (not air) sprays out
4. Close, restart boiler

## Other Pelham-specific causes

- **Stuck thermostatic radiator valve (TRV)** — common in 1980s renovations
- **Pitched wrong** — single-pipe radiators must slope toward the inlet; furniture or settled floors can change the pitch
- **Sediment in the riser** — Pelham's older cast-iron piping accumulates sludge
- **Closed isolation valve** — sometimes a contractor or prior owner closed a valve and forgot

## When to call a pro

- Boiler pressure dropping repeatedly (leak somewhere)
- Banging/water hammer that won't stop
- Radiator leaking from the body (cracked — replacement only)
- Multiple radiators cold (likely circulator pump or near-boiler piping)
