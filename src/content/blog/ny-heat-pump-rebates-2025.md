If you've Googled "heat pump rebate NY" you've seen a dozen programs and zero clear answers. Here's how the money actually works for a Westchester homeowner replacing oil or gas heat with a cold-climate heat pump.

## NYS Clean Heat (the big one)

Administered by your utility (Con Edison or NYSEG in most of Westchester). Pays the contractor directly — you see the discount on the install quote, not a check months later.

- **Air-source heat pump (whole home):** typically $2,000–$8,000 depending on heating capacity and whether you remove fossil-fuel backup.
- **Ground-source (geothermal):** significantly more, often $1,500–$2,000 per ton.
- **Partial-home / supplemental:** smaller, capped rebate.

## Federal 25C tax credit

A 30% federal tax credit on qualifying heat pumps, capped at **$2,000 per year**. Equipment must meet the CEE highest-tier efficiency for your region (Northern). Most cold-climate units we install qualify; check the AHRI certificate.

## Inflation Reduction Act HEEHRA rebates

NY's HEEHRA program (income-qualified) launched in 2024 and is rolling out by region. For households under 80% of area median income, up to **$8,000** off a heat pump install. 80–150% AMI gets 50% off. Above 150% AMI: federal credit only.

## A real Westchester example

A 2,200 sq ft home in Yonkers swapping an old oil boiler for a 4-ton cold-climate heat pump:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Install cost (typical) | $22,000 |
| NYS Clean Heat rebate | -$5,500 |
| Federal 25C credit | -$2,000 |
| **Net cost** | **~$14,500** |

Add another $3,000–$8,000 off if you qualify income-wise for HEEHRA.

## What to ask your installer

1. Are you a NYS Clean Heat participating contractor? (Required to get the rebate.)
2. Will the rebate be applied to my quote, or do I file?
3. What's the AHRI certificate number? (You'll need it for the tax credit.)
4. Is this a full replacement or hybrid/dual-fuel?
