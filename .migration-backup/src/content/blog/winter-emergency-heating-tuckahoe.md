Tuckahoe (10707) has a mix of small lots, condos, and dense single-family homes — meaning a no-heat night affects neighbors fast (frozen shared walls, burst pipes). Here's the safe playbook.

## Immediate steps when the heat fails

1. **Check the thermostat** — battery, mode (Heat not Cool), set point above current room temp
2. **Check the furnace switch** — usually red, near the unit or top of basement stairs
3. **Check the breaker** — reset once if tripped
4. **Hard reset the furnace** — wall switch off 60 seconds, back on
5. **Check for a tripped CO detector** — leave immediately if alarming

If none of that works, you're calling a tech. Now protect the house.

## Prevent frozen pipes (Tuckahoe priority)

- Open cabinet doors under kitchen and bathroom sinks (especially on exterior walls)
- Run a pencil-thin trickle from the highest faucet
- Open the basement faucet
- Close the garage door if there's plumbing in the wall
- Add temporary insulation (towels, foam) around exposed pipes near foundation vents

## Safe space heater use

- **Plug directly into a wall outlet** — never an extension cord (top cause of Tuckahoe winter house fires)
- **3 feet of clearance from anything flammable**
- **Off when you sleep or leave the house** — non-negotiable
- **Never use a gas oven or range for heat** — carbon monoxide kills

## What to set the thermostat to overnight

If heat is partially working but weak, set to **62°F minimum** to prevent pipe freeze in walls. Don't drop below 55°F even to save fuel — the recovery cost (and burst-pipe risk) isn't worth it.

## When to call us — and when to call 911

**Call Bravo (914) 318-7368:**
- No heat, no flames, no CO smell
- Furnace clicking but not igniting
- Strange burning smell from unit (not gas)

**Call 911 first:**
- Gas smell anywhere in the house
- CO detector alarming
- Visible flames outside the burn chamber
- Soot around the furnace
