Katonah (10536) and the rest of Northern Westchester see real winter — multiple sub-zero mornings each season, design temperature around 0°F, and occasional polar vortex events down to -10°F. The honest question: do heat pumps actually work here?

## Short answer: yes, if you spec the right unit

Modern **cold-climate heat pumps (ccHPs)** are rated to deliver 100% of their nameplate capacity at 5°F and meaningful heat down to -15°F. They are not the same as the heat pumps your parents had in 1995.

## What "cold-climate" means specifically

A ccHP must be on the **NEEP cold climate list** to qualify for NYSERDA Clean Heat rebates in Katonah. Key specs:
- Capacity at 5°F ≥ 70% of capacity at 47°F
- Modulating compressor (variable speed)
- Vapor injection or enhanced refrigerant cycle
- HSPF2 ≥ 8.1

Brands consistently delivering this: **Mitsubishi Hyper-Heat, Daikin Aurora, Fujitsu Halcyon XLTH, Bosch IDS 2.0, Carrier Infinity Greenspeed, Trane XV20i**.

## Real-world Katonah performance

We have multiple Katonah customers running pure heat-pump heat with no backup. In a 3,200 sq ft renovated colonial in Bedford Hills (right next door):
- 2024 lowest outdoor temp: -7°F
- House held setpoint of 68°F
- January electric bill: $487 (vs. $890 prior year on oil)

## When you should keep a backup

Honest cases where dual-fuel beats heat-pump-only in Katonah:
- Home > 5,000 sq ft with poor insulation
- Long power outages common (oil/gas backup independent of grid)
- Homeowner traveling and concerned about freeze-up
- Homes with limited electrical service (200A struggling with EV + heat pump + range)

## Sizing matters more than brand

Most "my heat pump can't keep up in cold weather" complaints we troubleshoot in Northern Westchester trace back to:
1. **Wrong unit selected** (non-cold-climate model installed in our climate)
2. **Undersized** (no Manual J done)
3. **Bad commissioning** (refrigerant charge off, defrost cycle misconfigured)
4. **Air sealing/insulation issues** (heat pump is the wrong fix — fix the envelope)
