Dobbs Ferry (10522) energy bills are some of the highest in Westchester — older homes, electric rates near $0.30/kWh, and hilly terrain that traps cold air. Here's the realistic playbook to cut bills.

## Free or near-free changes

**Lower the thermostat 3°F at night and away.** Each degree saves about 3% on heating costs. Programmable or smart thermostat does it automatically.

**Replace filters monthly.** Restricted airflow forces the system to run longer. The cheapest energy upgrade in HVAC.

**Open south-facing curtains in winter, close them in summer.** Free passive solar.

**Reverse ceiling fan direction.** Clockwise (downward push) in winter pulls warm air off the ceiling.

**Seal obvious air leaks.** Door sweeps, window weatherstripping, electrical outlet gaskets. $40 at Lowe's.

## Sub-$1,000 upgrades

**Smart thermostat ($150–$300 installed)** — pays back in 12–18 months in Dobbs Ferry.

**Air sealing + attic insulation top-up ($600–$1,200)** — biggest comfort change you can make to a Dobbs Ferry colonial. Often eligible for NYS Comfort Home rebates.

**Duct sealing ($500–$1,200)** — if you have ductwork, 20–30% of conditioned air leaks into unheated basement/attic. Mastic-sealing returns the leak.

## Mid-range upgrades ($2,000–$8,000)

**Heat pump water heater** — $3,800–$5,500 installed, $1,750 NYSERDA rebate. Cuts hot water energy use by 60–70%.

**Whole-house dehumidifier** — $2,800–$4,500. Lets you raise the AC setpoint 3–4°F at the same comfort level.

**Variable-speed blower retrofit** — $1,200–$2,000. Cuts blower electrical use by 60%+.

## Big upgrades ($15,000–$30,000)

**Switch from oil to a heat pump** — Dobbs Ferry has many oil-heat homes that pay $4,000–$7,000/year. A cold-climate heat pump cuts that by 50–65%, plus $5,000–$10,000 in NYS Clean Heat rebates.

**Insulation + air sealing + heat pump combo** — eligible for NYS Comfort Home incentive of $2,000–$5,000+.

## What doesn't actually save money in Dobbs Ferry

- "Energy-saving" portable space heaters
- Window film (negligible R-value)
- Solar attic fans (often pull conditioned air out of the house)
- Closing vents in unused rooms (raises duct static pressure, can damage equipment)
