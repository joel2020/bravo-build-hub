New Rochelle (10801, 10804, 10805) sits close enough to the Sound that salt air, summer humidity, and heavy oak pollen all wear on AC equipment faster than inland Westchester. A proper spring tune-up matters more here.

## The real 18-point AC tune-up

A legitimate tune-up takes 60–90 minutes and includes:

**Outdoor condenser**
1. Wash coil with low-pressure water + coil cleaner (salt air corrodes aluminum fins fast)
2. Straighten bent fins
3. Inspect and tighten electrical connections
4. Check capacitor microfarad rating (most fail in summer 3–5)
5. Measure compressor amp draw vs. nameplate
6. Verify refrigerant charge via superheat/subcool

**Indoor air handler / furnace**
7. Replace or clean filter
8. Inspect evaporator coil for biological growth (humid New Rochelle basements are coil-mold central)
9. Flush condensate drain line with vinegar + nitrogen
10. Test float switch
11. Tighten blower wheel set screw
12. Measure static pressure across the coil

**Controls and safety**
13. Test thermostat calibration
14. Test high/low pressure cutouts
15. Verify defrost board (heat pumps only)
16. Check disconnect and breaker condition
17. Inspect refrigerant line insulation (sun and squirrels destroy it)
18. Document supply/return temperature split (should be 18–22°F)

## Tune-up red flags in New Rochelle

If a contractor:
- Spends less than 45 minutes
- Doesn't open the air handler
- Adds refrigerant without finding the leak
- Quotes a "free" tune-up — they're hunting for upsells

…walk away.

## When to schedule

Best window for New Rochelle: **mid-March through mid-May**. By June, lead times stretch and pollen has already coated your coil for the season.
