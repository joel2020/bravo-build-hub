Ossining (10562) properties — particularly the older sections near Croton Aqueduct and the new construction in Briarcliff Manor — frequently include detached garages, workshops, or studios that someone now wants heated. Here's the realistic option breakdown.

## Quick recommendations by use case

| Use | Best option |
|---|---|
| Occasional garage warmth (winter projects) | Vented gas garage heater |
| Year-round workshop with cooling too | Ductless heat pump mini-split |
| Studio / office / yoga space | Ductless heat pump mini-split |
| Pool house | Ductless heat pump mini-split |
| Just keep above freezing for plumbing | Small electric heater on thermostat |

## Option 1 — Ductless heat pump mini-split (most popular)

**Cost:** $4,500–$8,500 for a single-zone install in an Ossining detached structure.
**Capacity:** 9,000–24,000 BTU covers most garages and workshops up to ~600 sq ft.
**Operating cost:** Lowest of any option, especially with cold-climate units that work down to -5°F.
**Bonus:** Provides AC for summer use.
**Rebate:** Yes — NYS Clean Heat, often $1,000–$2,000.

## Option 2 — Vented natural gas / propane garage heater

**Cost:** $2,500–$4,500 installed (gas line + heater + venting).
**Capacity:** 30,000–75,000 BTU.
**Pros:** Cheap to operate if natural gas is available; fast warm-up.
**Cons:** No AC; gas line trenching adds cost; combustion air requirements.
**Best for:** Mechanic's garage, infrequent use, no cooling needed.

## Option 3 — Infrared / radiant tube heater

**Cost:** $2,800–$5,500.
**Pros:** Heats objects (you, your tools) instead of air — efficient for tall spaces.
**Cons:** Doesn't provide ambient warmth; only useful when standing under it.
**Best for:** Auto shops, very tall workshop ceilings.

## Option 4 — Extend the main house boiler

**Cost:** $4,000–$10,000 depending on distance and trenching.
**Pros:** Uses existing boiler; quiet baseboard heat.
**Cons:** Trenching cost; freeze risk in lines; usually doesn't make economic sense unless you're already trenching for other utilities.

## Option 5 — Electric resistance heater on thermostat

**Cost:** $200–$600 unit + dedicated 240V circuit ($400–$1,200).
**Pros:** Simple, cheap install.
**Cons:** Most expensive to operate (3–4x heat pump). Only OK for occasional freeze-protection use.

## Insulation matters more than the heater choice

A 600 sq ft Ossining garage with R-13 walls and R-30 ceiling can be comfortably heated by a 12,000 BTU mini-split. The same garage uninsulated needs 36,000+ BTU and twice the operating cost. **Insulate first.**

## Ossining permits

Town of Ossining requires:
- Electrical permit for new dedicated circuits
- Plumbing/mechanical permit for gas extensions
- Building permit for major modifications
