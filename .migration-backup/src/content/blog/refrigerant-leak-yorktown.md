If your Yorktown Heights (10598) AC is blowing warm, freezing up, or recently needed a "refrigerant recharge," you almost certainly have a leak. Here's how a real diagnosis and repair works.

## Why topping off without finding the leak is a scam

Refrigerant doesn't get used up. If yours is low, it leaked. Adding more without finding the leak means:
- You'll be back next year (or sooner)
- Refrigerant is expensive ($90–$220/lb depending on type)
- Continuous low charge damages the compressor — a $2,500+ repair

EPA Section 608 requires leak detection on systems > 50 lbs, but ethical contractors do it on residential systems too.

## Yorktown leak diagnosis process

1. **Electronic leak detector** — finds 90% of leaks in minutes
2. **UV dye + black light** — for slow seeps
3. **Bubble test** — fittings and connections
4. **Pressure test with nitrogen** — confirms suspected location
5. **Document with photos** — for warranty and your records

A proper Yorktown leak diagnosis runs **$220–$385**. If we find and fix the leak in the same visit, we apply that to the repair.

## Common Yorktown AC leak locations

- **Outdoor coil** (wear, vibration, oxidation)
- **Indoor evaporator coil** (formicary corrosion — major problem in 2010–2018 vintage units)
- **Refrigerant line set** (mechanical damage, especially where it enters the house)
- **Schrader valve cores** (cheap to replace)
- **Brazed fittings** (poor original install)

## R-22 systems in Yorktown — bad news

If your Yorktown AC is pre-2010 and uses R-22 ("Freon"), repairs are often economically irrational. R-22 has been phased out; only recovered/recycled supply remains, at premium prices. For most R-22 systems with leaks, replacement is cheaper than repair within 2 years.

## Replacement vs. repair decision rule

- Repair cost > 30% of replacement → consider new system
- System > 12 years old → strongly consider replacement
- Multiple leak points → replace
- R-22 system → replace
