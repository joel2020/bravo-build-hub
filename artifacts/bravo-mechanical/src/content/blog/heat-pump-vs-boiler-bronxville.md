Bronxville (10708) is one of the trickiest ZIP codes in Westchester for HVAC decisions. The village is dominated by 1920s–1940s Tudor, colonial, and stucco homes with steam or hot-water radiators, no ductwork, and tight utility rooms. Here's the honest comparison.

## The Bronxville reality check

Most pre-war Bronxville homes have:
- Cast-iron radiators (steam or hot water)
- No central AC ductwork
- Limited basement headroom
- Historic district restrictions on exterior equipment placement

That changes the heat-pump math significantly.

## Option 1 — Replace the boiler in-kind

**Cost:** $11,000–$16,000 for a high-efficiency gas boiler.
**Pros:** Keeps existing radiators, fast install, no electrical work, no permits beyond plumbing.
**Cons:** No AC, locks you into gas for another 20 years, no NYS Clean Heat rebate.

## Option 2 — Ducted heat pump (full conversion)

**Cost:** $35,000–$60,000+ in a typical Bronxville home (ductwork is the killer).
**Pros:** Heating + cooling in one system, qualifies for $5,000–$10,000 in Clean Heat rebates.
**Cons:** Major construction, soffit drops, lost closet space, often impractical in historic homes.

## Option 3 — Ductless mini-split heat pump (best for most Bronxville homes)

**Cost:** $18,000–$32,000 for a 4–6 zone system.
**Pros:** No ductwork, minor exterior penetrations, full Clean Heat rebate eligibility, handles Bronxville winters down to -5°F with cold-climate units.
**Cons:** Wall units aren't to everyone's taste; you may keep the boiler as backup.

## Option 4 — Hybrid (heat pump + existing boiler)

**Cost:** $20,000–$35,000.
**Pros:** Heat pump runs above 30°F (cheapest mode), boiler kicks in below. Best of both worlds.
**Cons:** More controls complexity; needs a tech who knows hybrid commissioning.

## What we recommend for most Bronxville homes

For a 2,500–3,500 sq ft home in Lawrence Park or Cedar Knoll, a **6-zone ductless heat pump with the existing boiler kept as winter backup** is usually the best cost/comfort/rebate balance. Total install runs $22,000–$28,000 before rebates, dropping to $15,000–$20,000 after Clean Heat and federal 25C credits.
