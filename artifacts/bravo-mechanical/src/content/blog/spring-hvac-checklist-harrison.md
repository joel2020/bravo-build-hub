Harrison, Purchase, and West Harrison (10528, 10577, 10604) homes tend toward larger lots, long refrigerant runs, pool houses, and detached garages — all of which need attention before AC season.

## 1. Clear the outdoor condenser

Harrison's tree canopy means leaves, oak pollen, and seed pods pile against condenser fins. Cut back vegetation to **3 feet of clearance** on all sides and 5 feet overhead.

## 2. Wash the coil

Use a garden hose (low pressure, not a pressure washer) and an inexpensive coil cleaner from Home Depot. Spray top-down, let dwell 5 minutes, rinse. Restores 5–15% efficiency.

## 3. Replace filters everywhere

- Main HVAC filter — every 2–3 months in Harrison spring/summer (pollen heavy)
- Pool house mini-split filters — pull and rinse monthly during pool season
- Detached garage / workshop split — clean before May

## 4. Check the thermostat schedule

Switch from heat to cool, set the schedule, and verify the cooling setpoint is reasonable (75–78°F is ideal for efficiency in Harrison's climate).

## 5. Look for ice or oil residue on refrigerant lines

Both indicate a refrigerant leak — call before running the system or you'll burn out the compressor.

## 6. Test condensate drain

Pour a cup of distilled water down the drain pan. It should disappear within seconds. If not, the line is clogged — flush with vinegar + nitrogen, or call us.

## 7. Inspect the disconnect box outside

Squirrels and chipmunks love the warm relays. Pull the disconnect handle, look for nests/wires, reseat.

## 8. Schedule a professional tune-up

DIY gets you 70% of the way. A pro tune-up (March–April booking) catches:
- Refrigerant charge issues
- Capacitor degradation (most fail in the first 95°F day of summer)
- Contactor pitting
- Blower motor amp draw

## Harrison-specific extras

- **Pool house mini-splits** — annual coil cleaning is essential due to chlorine air migration
- **Long refrigerant line sets** — Purchase estate homes often have 60–100ft line sets that need pressure testing every 5 years
- **Generator-tied HVAC** — verify your AC can start on generator power (LRA matters)
