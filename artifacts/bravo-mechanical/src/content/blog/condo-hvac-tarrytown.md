Tarrytown (10591) and Sleepy Hollow have a growing condo and co-op market — including buildings along Main Street, the Hudson, and the Edge-on-Hudson development. HVAC replacement here has more rules than single-family.

## First — read your offering plan and house rules

Most Tarrytown condos and co-ops require:
- Board approval before any HVAC work
- Licensed/insured contractor with current Westchester license + COI naming the building
- Permit pulled with Village of Tarrytown
- Use of building-approved equipment locations

We pull all of this for you, but the board sign-off has to come from you 2–6 weeks ahead.

## Common Tarrytown condo HVAC types

### Through-the-wall PTAC units

The "hotel-style" units in 1970s–1990s buildings. Replacement is straightforward (~$1,800–$2,800 installed) and rarely needs board approval beyond a notification.

### Vertical fan-coil with central chiller/boiler

Common in newer luxury buildings. The fan-coil is yours; the chiller/boiler is shared. Replacement of the fan-coil only is your responsibility — $2,500–$5,000 installed.

### Heat pump split (indoor + outdoor unit)

Trickier — outdoor unit placement requires board approval, and many Tarrytown buildings restrict balcony or roof installations.

### Ductless mini-split

Increasingly popular for older Tarrytown brownstones converted to condos. Outdoor unit placement is the main hurdle. Cost: $7,500–$18,000 depending on zones.

## What needs board approval (almost always)

- Any exterior unit visible from common areas
- Any penetration through an exterior wall
- Refrigerant line set routing through common spaces
- Electrical work that pulls from house meter
- Noise above building's published limits

## What usually doesn't need board approval

- In-kind PTAC replacement
- Fan-coil swap inside the unit
- Thermostat replacement
- Filter and maintenance work

## Tarrytown rebates that apply to condos

- **Con Edison heat pump rebate** — fully applies
- **NYS Clean Heat** — applies, including for ductless
- **Federal 25C tax credit** — applies (up to $2,000 heat pumps)

## Common condo HVAC mistakes to avoid

- Hiring an unlicensed contractor — voids HOA insurance
- Oversizing replacement — condos are usually well-insulated, original spec is often too big
- Skipping the load calc — leads to humidity issues
- Routing line sets through unauthorized common areas
