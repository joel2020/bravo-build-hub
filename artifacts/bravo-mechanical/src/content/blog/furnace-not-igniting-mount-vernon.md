We get more no-heat calls from Mount Vernon (10550, 10552, 10553) than almost any other Westchester ZIP — partly because the housing stock is older, partly because thermostat batteries die in cold snaps. Try these before booking a tech.

## 1. Check the thermostat batteries

If your screen is dim, blank, or showing "low batt," replace the AAs. This is the #1 false alarm we see in Fleetwood and South Side.

## 2. Confirm the furnace switch is on

Mount Vernon furnaces almost always have a wall switch that looks like a light switch — usually red, sometimes mounted at the top of the basement stairs. If a kid or contractor flipped it, no heat.

## 3. Check the breaker

Look for a tripped 15A or 20A breaker labeled "furnace" or "boiler." Reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call us.

## 4. Inspect the air filter

A filter that's been in for 6+ months can choke airflow and trigger the high-limit safety, locking out ignition. Pull it out and try a cycle without one (briefly) to test.

## 5. Check the condensate drain (high-efficiency furnaces only)

If you have a 90%+ AFUE furnace and see water pooling near the unit, the condensate trap or pump may have failed — most furnaces lock out when the condensate switch trips.

## 6. Reset the unit

Turn the wall switch off for 60 seconds, then back on. Many modern furnaces require a hard reset after 3 failed ignition attempts.

## 7. Check gas service

Is the gas stove working? If no — call Con Edison, not us. If yes but the furnace won't fire, the gas valve solenoid or igniter has likely failed.

## When to call Bravo

If you've tried all 7 and you're still cold:
- Cracked heat exchanger (carbon monoxide risk — leave the house)
- Failed inducer motor
- Failed igniter or flame sensor (most common, $150–$350 fix)
- Failed gas valve ($350–$650)
